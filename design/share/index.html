ATA

Arcane Transit Authority

A planet-wide city with no room to spare, so run your rails through earth, water, fire and air.

  • 3D, in the browser
  • Computer and phone
  • Building and management
  • One planet · 6 regions
  • 7 elemental trains
  • 30 to 45 minutes a term

The pitch

Run the trains of a planet with no room left

You're the Chief Engineer of the Arcane Transit Authority on Oikumene, a planet covered pole to pole in a single glittering city. Tap two places and the game deals you route cards: across the rooftops (if you pay to clear the buildings), between mountain peaks, along the sea floor, through the rock, straight through the molten core, or around the planet on an orbital ring.

Pick one and a glowing ley-rail, a rail of arcane energy in that train's colour, draws itself into place. An elemental train rides it straight through the sky, the sea, the rock or the magma: each is an advanced machine built to harness its element and survive inside it.

Every district's goods flow through your network to the Warp Terminal, where deep space trains warp them to the Homeworld at the centre of the galaxy. The Homeworld is a light in the sky you will never visit. Each game is one term as Chief Engineer under a Homeworld Charter: meet its quotas, survive the disasters, turn demolished corridors into parks, and leave the planet humming like a perfect machine. Commend all six Charters and the Homeworld answers.

The fantasy: you're the genius engineer who looks at a planet with no room for a single new rail and says "fine, we'll go through the core". It's the same planet every term, your planet, and you learn its seas, peaks and rift like a home city.

What was asked for

Every idea in the original pitch, and where it lives

  • the whole planet's covered in the city One planet, Oikumene: about 640 hexagonal districts on a globe you spin and zoom from orbit down to the streets. Its geography is the same every game; variety comes from its six regions, a fresh term each game, and six Homeworld Charters.
  • you have to clear buildings which is really expensive Surface lines must demolish every block they cross. Dense blocks cost more, and they stop making riders.
  • Earth train … right through the core … or at least through some mountains Earth trains bore their own straight tunnels through the rock, and shafts down to the core.
  • the core is too hot so you also need fire trains Earth goes down a shaft, fire crosses the core, earth comes up the far side. The game builds all three as one route card.
  • from the top of Mount Everest to the top of Katahdin Sky trains ride arcs of light from peak to peak.
  • water trains that go under the ocean … Atlantis Water trains glide along the sea floor in their own bubbles, between glass-domed undersea cities.
  • space trains that go around the world An orbital ring with space elevators down to the city.
  • deep space trains that warp away … you don't handle their other sides They warp your exports away and come back with short dispatches. Neither their destinations nor the Homeworld is ever shown, only heard.
  • 3D wormy … kind of trains Articulated trains whose linked cars bend smoothly along every curve.
  • if you had magic trains Seven elemental trains, one per element, each with its own clear colour, riding glowing ley-rails straight through the sky, sea, rock and magma.
  • a city … that Robotnik would love An original neon machine megacity of megablocks, domes, cables and smokestacks.
  • maximize the throughput/efficiency of the Homeworld Throughput (exports launched a minute) is the score that matters, against three quotas in each Charter.
  • buy and demolish buildings … add parks, trees, track-side attractions Demolish any block, then plant groves and parks, or build a Giant Wheel, a Fountain Plaza or a Monument to the Engineers.
  • Overall victory is a job well done Meet Quota III and the Charter is Commended. Commend all six and the Homeworld answers.
  • random disasters Seven kinds, each with a warning and a choice: Brace, Reroute or Repair.
  • stations … interchanges … earth has to hand off to fire Stations serving several layers become interchanges, and the Core Exchange is where earth hands off to fire.
  • a tech tree with upgrades per type of train Seven branches of four upgrades each.
  • milestones … random so each game has a slightly different nudge Three milestone cards are dealt each term from a pool of 30.
  • open them up roguelike style, buying them between games Each term earns Merits, which buy new milestone cards, starting perks and liveries.

How it plays

Tap two places, compare the route cards, watch it go

  1. Tap a place, a station or any district. The planet turns see-through so you can reach the far side, or you can pick from a list.
  2. Tap another. Route cards appear, one for each way to join them.
  3. Compare. Each card shows its cost, how many people and goods a minute it carries, and how much it adds to your exports. The path is previewed on the globe, with any blocks it would demolish outlined.
  4. Pick one. Every station, shaft and interchange it needs is built, the ley-rail draws itself across the world, and the first train glides out.
  5. Read the flow. Busy rails glow wider and pulse faster, full stretches flash, exports stream to the Warp Terminal as dots, and tapping the Throughput meter names the bottleneck.

The loops

Ten seconds: tap, tap, compare, build. One minute: fix the bottleneck the game names by adding a train, upgrading an interchange, buying a tech, building a second route, or clearing a block for a park. One term (30 to 45 minutes): grow from your starting region to the whole world and meet the Charter's Quota I, II and III before the Homeworld's Audit. Between terms: spend Merits, then pick a Charter and a starting region.

Ride any line: a chase camera follows a train along its rail, down shafts and through the core. The title screen rides random lines by itself.

The seven trains

Seven elemental trains, one element and one colour each

Each train is an advanced machine built around its element: arcane engineering fused with cyberpunk technology. Its linked cars bend smoothly along curves, and it harnesses its element to travel straight through it. You can tell them apart by look and colour alone. These are concept pictures; each becomes a 3D model, with its element added in the game as moving effects.

Surface · lightning

Rides a chrome maglev rail with a glowing seam, laid through the corridor you demolished. Fast and cheap to run.

Sky · wind

Rides arcs of light between mountain peaks, wrapped in ribbons of wind. Cheapest to build, but peaks only. Watch for meteors.

Water · the deep

Glides along the sea floor in its own bubble of swirling water. The only way to reach the rich Atlantis domes. Seaquakes stop it.

Earth · stone and crystal

Bores its own green-crystal-lined tunnels, straight under anything. Slow, and only as deep as the crust allows. Cave-ins happen.

Fire · magma

Swims through the open lava of the core between two Core Exchanges: the far side of the planet for a fair price. Core flares overheat it.

Space · stars

Rides the orbital ring, reached by space elevators near the equator. Fastest and biggest. Solar storms slow it.

Deep space · the void

No rail: it warps from the Warp Terminal's launch ring every 30 seconds with your exports, and returns with a dispatch.

Ley-rails

Each train runs straight through its element

Every line is a ley-rail, a glowing rail of arcane energy in its train's colour. From orbit, the ley-rails are your network in seven colours. Up close, each train rides through its world in the open.

Stations and places

Where the layers meet

A station that serves more than one layer becomes an interchange: each added layer's module stacks on top, with a glowing ring that fills as people and goods change trains. A full ring flashes and the game names it as the bottleneck. Stations next to each other are joined by walkways automatically, so a pier joins the coastal district behind it.

The Core Exchange is the heart of the idea: a Bore Station sinks a shaft to the core, earth trains hand off to fire trains, and two Core Exchanges get you to the far side of the world.

The inverted ecumenopolis

Demolish, then build it back greener

Buy and demolish any block, or a whole line of blocks. The buildings sink in a cloud of dust. On cleared ground, plant tree groves and parks, which make nearby districts busier. Next to a line, build a trackside attraction (a Giant Wheel, a Fountain Plaza or a Monument to the Engineers) for a big boost to riders within two districts.

Greenery is one of the three stars you're graded on, alongside meeting the quota in time and finishing richer than you started.

The planet and the Charters

One planet, six regions, six Homeworld Charters

Oikumene ("the inhabited world") is the only planet. Its geography never changes, so you learn it term after term. It has six regions you can start from, and the Far Wards: the ordinary city hinterland around the Warp Terminal, which stands on the equator 160° around the world from the Capital.

Each term, a new seed nudges how busy each district is, marks two or three priority districts in other parts of the world that export half as much again once you reach them, deals you milestones and disasters, and sometimes hands you a small network left by the previous Chief Engineer.

The Charter ladder

Each term is played under one of six Homeworld Charters, stricter each time: higher quotas, tighter deadlines, less money, more disasters. Each Charter also has a mandate, one rule that changes what you build. Commending a Charter (holding its Quota III) unlocks the next Charter and a new starting region. Any Charter can be replayed from any unlocked region.

The finale

Commend Charter VI and the Homeworld answers: every line pulses, a beam fires from the Warp Terminal to the galaxy's heart, and the Warp Conductor's last dispatch finally says where the trains go. You never travel there. After that, a Galactic Charter and free play remain.

Milestones, dealt at random

Each term deals three cards from your pool. Finish one and it flips over with a bonus. The starting ten include Long Haul (track once around the planet, 31,400 miles), Sky Fleet, Core Commuter, Green Belt, Atlantean Friend, Rush Hour, Ring of Fire, Clean Sweep, Interchange Master and Steady Exports. Twenty more can be unlocked, such as Every Peak, Untouchable and Half Way Round.

Unlocks between terms

Every term, won or lost, earns Merits for quotas, milestones and stars. The Merit Office sells new milestone cards for the pool, starting perks (extra money, a free tech, a free Core Exchange pair, an elevator already built, a reroll) and cosmetic train liveries. New Charters and starting regions unlock by Commending.

Disasters

Fifteen seconds' warning, then a choice

A banner with Juno's face points at what's at risk, and fast-forward drops back to normal speed. Brace pays half the repair price now to halve the damage. Reroute closes the line early, so nobody aboard is lost. Or gamble, and Repair all afterwards. Each disaster has a home region it strikes first.

Story

Dispatches from beyond the warp

Each returning deep space train brings two or three lines from somewhere you'll never see: a light, funny, slightly melancholy picture of a galaxy run on throughput. There are about sixty, building through the Charters toward the finale, where the Conductor finally says where the trains go.

Dispatcher Juno Brasswick

An ATA dispatcher and your cheerful assistant: the tutorial, hints and disaster warnings.

Auditor Ossian Thrumm

Homeworld Office of Throughput. Dry, exacting, secretly fond of you. Quotas, deadlines and grades.

The Warp Conductor

A mysterious voice who rides the deep space trains and sends the dispatches.

Overheard: a planet of gardens that wants to know what a "building" is · a world where all the trains run backwards · a station at the galaxy's edge where one passenger has waited a thousand years.

Look and sound

Elemental trains in a neon cyberpunk megacity

A dense neon megacity at night: chrome and gunmetal megablocks, cables and glowing conduits, holographic light shapes, wet reflective streets and a rose and deep-teal haze, with puffing smokestacks and warm lit windows. Through it all run the ley-rails and their elemental trains. It leans cyberpunk but stays playful and easy to read. The world's own colours are kept clear of the seven train colours, so lines never vanish into the scenery.

Surface · yellowSky · ice-cyanWater · deep blueEarth · greenFire · red-orangeSpace · violetDeep space · magenta

Big moments: rails draw themselves across the globe as trains glide out along them. On your first trip through the core, the camera dives down a crystal bore, through the lava with the fire train and up the far side. When a quota is met, a beam fires from the Warp Terminal to the galaxy's heart. When a Charter is Commended, every line pulses around the world and fireworks go up over the cities.

Sound: wondrous synthwave pads and strings on the title screen, an upbeat synthwave groove with a playful cyberpunk shimmer while you build (with a busier version as things heat up), a tense bed during disasters, and a fanfare for a job well done. Every action has a sound, and each train has its own voice: crackle, gust, bubble-glide, grinding rumble, roar, star-chime hum and warp zap.

Hardest parts

Keeping the flow of people and goods fast, readable and repeatable. Drawing clean ley-rails through every layer, from the sea floor to the core to orbit, and making each train's element read clearly in motion. Keeping a whole planet-city smooth on phones. Making the see-through view clear. Proving every Charter can actually be won: a helper program plays each Charter to find a winning plan, and the game replays it with real taps and clicks.
